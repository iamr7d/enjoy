A different question

What happens when a model has to look for itself?

Most models predict human gaze: they are handed a frame a person reached, and scored on where that person looked. They are never asked to survive their own mistakes.

JOY is given the frame only through a foveated percept, computes its evidence on that percept and never on the full image, arbitrates between competing cues, samples a fixation, and begins again from wherever it landed. The four panels are one real frame through those four stages.

Research at a glance

Four constraints, one loop

Human-like vision

Acuity falls with eccentricity, and both evidence models see only what survives it.

Competing guidance

Salience, semantics, oculomotor history and a centre bias, each with its own weight.

Closed-loop control

It updates its own history from its own choices. No human fixation reaches inference.

Held-out evaluation

Free film viewing and task-directed search, paired and bootstrapped, folds reported.

Featured results

A model got better at predicting gaze without getting better at looking

Prediction versus observation

+0.0099 bits as a predictor, positive in five of five folds. As an observer, a median of −0.919 and three wins in eight clips.

Where the weight actually goes

A global weight set fitted for held-out likelihood gives the semantic map zero in four of five folds, while per-frame fits give it 0.0475.

Temporal behaviour

Fixations of 214 to 220 ms against a human 179, saccades of 2.4 to 4.7 degrees against 1.44. The gap no semantic mechanism touched.

Where the evidence stops

A page that reports only what worked is not reporting a result. Adaptive arbitration is constrained here, not refuted. Foveation was not shown to humanise the viewer. The semantic map may be operating through high-level image structure rather than meaning. Narrative memory failed outright, and the claim that it helped was withdrawn. The closed-loop comparisons use eight clips and are underpowered.
